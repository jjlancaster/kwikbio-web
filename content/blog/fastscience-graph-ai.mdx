The instinct in 2026 is to throw a bigger language model at any research problem. That instinct is wrong, and it costs you weeks of compute.

Here's the stack we actually use, and why each layer is non-negotiable.

## Layer 1: Neo4j as the causal backbone

We don't store papers — we store **entities and causal arcs** extracted from papers. A typical kwiKBio knowledge graph for a single domain (say, microbial bioenergy) contains ~50,000 entities and ~200,000 typed arcs. Arc types matter: `regulates`, `inhibits`, `co-occurs-with`, and `causally-precedes` are not interchangeable.

Neo4j is the right database here because **path queries are first-class**. The questions we ask — "what regulates X two hops away from Y?" — are 5-line Cypher queries on Neo4j and 50-line monstrosities on Postgres.

## Layer 2: SLAM as the discovery layer

Sub-Linear Association Mining (Evan Steeg, US 6,493,637) was designed to find higher-order correlations in multidimensional data — the kind that pair-wise statistics miss entirely.

We use SLAM to discover candidate arcs the literature hasn't yet annotated. The output is always a **hypothesis**, never a claim. SLAM surfaces "these three entities co-cluster in 87% of relevant studies"; the rest of the pipeline tests whether that co-clustering is causally meaningful.

## Layer 3: PRISM-9 reduction

Most well-posed research problems have a remarkable property: they're **dominated by ≤9 entities**. The other 49,991 nodes in the graph contribute noise.

PRISM-9 forces that reduction. It picks the 9 entities whose joint variance explains the most outcome variance, then prunes everything else for the current query. This is the step that makes the next layer tractable.

## Layer 4: Cube-27 expansion

Each of the 9 dominant entities expands fractally into 3 sub-factors, giving a 3×3×3 cube of 27 leaf variables. The geometry isn't arbitrary — it preserves causal locality, so adjacent cells in the cube correspond to causally adjacent sub-factors.

## Layer 5: QUBO solving

With 27 binary variables (active / inactive sub-factor), path planning becomes a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization. That's a problem class quantum annealers (D-Wave Advantage at 5,000+ qubits) solve directly.

We get an optimal causal path in **seconds** on quantum hardware. Classical simulated annealing handles it in **minutes** when quantum capacity is unavailable. Either way, the user sees ranked experiments before they finish their coffee.

## Why not just bigger LLMs?

Because LLMs hallucinate causal arcs, and you can't ground them in your specific domain manual without RAG plumbing that costs more than the model. The graph layer eliminates a class of failure modes that no amount of pre-training fixes.

LLMs are still in the loop — they're our natural-language interface and our summarizer. But they don't drive the science.
