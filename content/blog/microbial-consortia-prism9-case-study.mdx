When we picked the first vertical slice for FastScience! v7, we deliberately chose a problem with three properties: large literature, real economic stakes, and a working researcher willing to validate every claim.

We landed on **microbial consortia bioenergy** — specifically, two-organism consortia for methane yield from agricultural digestate.

## The problem

Anaerobic digestion of corn-stover slurry produces methane. Yield varies by 4× across reported studies, and nobody fully understands why. Single-organism cultures underperform; two-organism consortia sometimes win dramatically and sometimes collapse.

Hypothesis: there's a small set of dominant variables — substrate composition, dominant species pair, retention time, micronutrient availability, pH buffering — that explain most of the variance.

## What the graph looked like

After ingesting the relevant literature (~3,200 papers from PubMed + AGRICOLA, plus the USDA Anaerobic Digester registry), our Neo4j graph held:

- **14,372 entities** — organisms, substrates, intermediates, environmental conditions, observed outcomes.
- **51,884 typed arcs** — `metabolizes`, `inhibits`, `co-occurs-with`, `produces`.
- **214 distinct outcome metrics** — methane yield, VFA accumulation, biomass production, etc.

## What PRISM-9 picked

PRISM-9 reduced 14,372 entities to these 9 dominant factors for the question "maximize methane yield from corn-stover digestate":

1. Dominant primary degrader genus (*Clostridium* vs *Ruminococcus*)
2. Secondary methanogen species (*Methanosarcina* vs *Methanosaeta*)
3. C:N ratio of input substrate
4. Hydraulic retention time
5. Operating pH (buffered vs unbuffered)
6. Trace-element supplementation (Ni / Co availability)
7. Inoculum source (cow rumen vs municipal digester sludge)
8. Temperature regime (mesophilic vs thermophilic)
9. Pre-treatment of substrate (none vs steam-explosion vs alkaline)

Notably absent: total VFA load, ammonia concentration, mixing rate. PRISM-9 deemed these correlated-but-not-causal for this specific question.

## What Cube-27 surfaced

The 9 factors fanned out into 27 leaf variables. The QUBO solver found one strongly causally coherent configuration:

> ***Ruminococcus albus* + *Methanosarcina barkeri*, mesophilic, C:N ≈ 25, retention 18d, buffered to pH 7.0, Ni-supplemented, rumen inoculum, steam-exploded substrate.***

## The experiment we proposed

The ARS engine emitted a 4-arm comparative trial: the optimal configuration above, two single-variable perturbations (swap methanogen, swap pre-treatment), and an industry-standard control. Estimated cost: $48K. Estimated time to results: 6 weeks. Matched CROs (USDA-registered): 7.

Total time from question to ranked experiment proposal: **97 minutes**, including domain-manual review and human sign-off.

## What's next

The trial is in arm 2 as of this writing. Whatever the outcome, the SSKM updates, and the next user querying "microbial bioenergy" inherits the refined causal model.

That's FastScience! v7 working as designed.
